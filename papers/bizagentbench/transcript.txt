BizAgentBench: Unveiling Hidden Challenges in Deploying Long-Horizon Agents for Real-World Business

1 / Why the ending is insufficient
An agent can finish a payment workflow with the right final balance and still have broken an important rule halfway through. BizAgentBench makes that distinction testable. Its central mechanism checks the entire execution history, not just the ending.

Imagine an account containing sixty dollars that must always keep twenty dollars in reserve. The agent pays a sixty-dollar retainer, then later receives money and restores the balance. The ending looks acceptable. The earlier zero balance still violates the requirement. This is an actual failure pattern reported in the paper's simulated environment.

2 / Environment and scoring
The benchmark scores three things. Did the agent achieve the customer's goal? Did it follow the required process? And did it protect specified records and avoid prohibited side effects? Strict success requires all three. These checks run as deterministic code over states, receipts and ordered events. A language model does not decide the final score.

The environment reconstructs payment workflows from Ant International's business procedures. Sensitive details are rewritten into synthetic records. There are fifty tasks, seven hundred eighty-nine knowledge documents, and six hundred one discoverable business capabilities. The agent reaches these through nine always-available tools, rather than receiving hundreds of tool definitions at once.

A separate model plays the customer. It reveals facts, supplies documents and approves specific proposals. The tested agent must ask useful questions, identify which policy version applies, discover the necessary operations, and coordinate actions that share money or deadlines. A permitted action now can consume resources needed later.

3 / Building a valid benchmark
The construction diagram matters here. Acceptance rules are defined before reference runs. Passing reference traces show that each task can be solved. Alternative valid paths and deliberate violations test the evaluator, and researchers and business experts review the cases. The agent does not have to imitate one prescribed sequence.

This extends an existing research direction. Tau-squared Bench studies agents and users acting in a shared environment. Tau-Knowledge combines banking workflows with a large document collection. BizAgentBench emphasizes whether consequential requirements remain satisfied at the moment each action happens. It introduces an evaluation framework, not a new model-training loss.

4 / Measured success and repeatability
Look at the results table. Claude Opus 5 achieves sixty-four percent strict success, and GPT-6 Sol achieves 61.3 percent. Those are observed results, not a statistically established ranking: Opus has one run per task, while Sol has three. Sol succeeds in all three repetitions on only forty percent of tasks.

5 / Process and information gaps
For Qwen3.8-27B, goal attainment is forty-two percent, but strict success falls to 32.7 percent. That gap illustrates why the process checks matter. A rejected premature payment attempt also counts as a process violation, even when no unauthorized transfer actually occurs.

The information ablation offers a useful clue. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash starts at 43.3 percent strict success. Giving it the applicable documents raises that to fifty percent. Giving it all customer-known facts upfront raises it to seventy percent. Gathering the right facts and retaining their terms appear especially difficult.

6 / Resource use and limits
Long traces also cost effort. That model averages 191 tool calls against about 112 in the reference traces, largely from additional searches and queries. The reference paths are not proven optimal, and tool counts are not dollar costs.

This remains a small, synthetic preprint benchmark. It does not certify real-world financial safety. Its practical value is a sharper test: did the agent achieve the requested result through actions that stayed valid throughout?
