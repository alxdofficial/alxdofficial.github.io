Waypoint-OPD: Transferring RL-Induced Policy Shifts via Gated On-Policy Distillation

Three saved teacher versions weight stable preference changes during student training.

THE TAKEAWAY

A teacher’s learning path can identify which changes deserve transfer.

KEEP IN MIND

Same-family model assumptions and checkpoint selection limit generalization of reported gains.

READ / COMPLETE NARRATION

The transcript.

Waypoint starts with a student generating its own answer. At each partial answer, three saved versions of a teacher score the student's likely next words. The teacher before learning, halfway through learning, and after learning reveal whether its preferences moved consistently. This matters because copying the final teacher can copy its weaknesses. Even copying only the difference between its first and final versions can hide reversals during training. Waypoint uses the middle snapshot to judge which final changes deserve weight, without making that middle model the imitation target.

The student proposes its sixteen most probable next tokens, the small text units a language model predicts. Their probabilities become weights. Each frozen teacher scores exactly those candidates. The method subtracts the weighted average from each early and late change, so it compares relative preferences rather than a shift shared by every candidate. The experiments use the Qwen3 language-model family, with a four-billion-parameter teacher saved before reinforcement learning, at six hundred training steps, and at twelve hundred. Reinforcement learning here means improving answers through rewarded training.

Next, the method measures the angle between those centered early and late changes. Changes pointing together retain the teaching signal; opposing directions switch it off. A special near-zero case keeps the signal rather than dividing by an unstable norm. A batch-level rescaling prevents the average gate from becoming too small. The weighted final-minus-initial change then trains the student, while a penalty keeps its distribution near its starting point. The extra ingredient costs one additional frozen-teacher scoring pass. Agreement remains a heuristic: a teacher can consistently learn the wrong preference.

The paper tests three student sizes on seven competition-mathematics benchmarks. They include the American Invitational Mathematics Examination, the Harvard–MIT Mathematics Tournament, and American Mathematics Competitions. Its score averages correctness across thirty-two sampled answers per question, then gives each benchmark equal weight. This is not the chance that at least one answer succeeds. Against endpoint-only Direct On-Policy Distillation, the eight-billion-parameter student rises from 53.96 to 56.14 percent. The fourteen-billion student rises from 57.61 to 59.20, and the thirty-billion mixture-of-experts student from 55.96 to 57.85.

Code evaluation combines short Python-programming problems, expanded correctness tests, and the LiveCodeBench coding benchmark. Here, the metric averages correctness over eight generated answers, then averages five evaluation scores. For the same three student sizes, endpoint-only scores of 50.59, 56.30, and 56.35 rise to 53.25, 57.82, and 57.54. Waypoint wins the domain average in all six mathematics-and-code settings. That does not mean it wins every individual benchmark against every alternative. The larger students can outperform the final teacher because they transfer its improvement rather than imitate its entire behavior.

On the eight-billion-parameter mathematics student, removing the gate lowers the average from 56.14 to 53.50. Shuffling gates across prefixes lowers it further, to 51.20. These controls support assigning the right gate to the right context. But checkpoint selection uses the 2024 American Invitational Mathematics Examination, which also appears in the reported mathematics average, so that average is not fully held out. All students share one model family and tokenizer. The practical lesson is to retain intermediate teacher checkpoints, then test the transfer on disjoint validation data before claiming a general recipe.
